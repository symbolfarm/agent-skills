# Polish review

Tests say whether a product works. A polish review says whether it feels
finished. Use a judge with a fresh context, preferably a different agent from
the builder, given only what a user would see: screenshots at phone and
desktop sizes, a short screen recording or a step-by-step capture of the main
flows, and the brief's reference look if it has one. Do not give it the code.

## Rubric

Score each item 1–5, with one sentence of evidence and the single change that
would raise the score most.

1. **First minute.** A newcomer understands what this is and what to do, and
   wants to keep going.
2. **Visual consistency.** One coherent style: type, colour, spacing, iconography
   and motion agree. It matches the reference look where one was given.
3. **Hierarchy and layout.** The important thing is the most prominent. No dead
   space, no crowding, and it holds at phone and desktop widths.
4. **Feedback.** Every action visibly does something. Waits show progress.
   Refusals and errors say why, and what to do next.
5. **States.** Empty, loading, error, offline and first-run states are designed,
   not left as defaults.
6. **Delight.** Animation, sound or small touches that make it pleasant, without
   getting in the way. Reduced-motion preferences are respected.
7. **Copy.** Words are short, consistent in tone, and name things the same way
   everywhere.
8. **Accessibility.** Keyboard and screen-reader use work, contrast is adequate,
   and touch targets are big enough.

## Using the scores

The build is aiming for 4 or more on every item. Low scores join the backlog
and are ranked like any other item. Re-judge after fixes, with a fresh context
again, so the judge is not anchored on its earlier scores. Put the final scores
and the judge's identity on the handover. A score is evidence about the
product, not a target to argue with: if you disagree, record why in
`DECISIONS.md`.
